01 — THE RIGHT TO CREATE
THE PETITION

Micronationalism was built on the freedom to create.

The Charter for the Ethical Use of Artificial Intelligence in Micronationalism recognises the value of responsible AI. But its Article V makes a categorical claim: where a project is focused entirely on developing a country in a virtual environment, it is geofiction, not micronationalism.

We disagree with turning one understanding of micronationalism into a standard for everyone. Micronations can be physical, digital, hybrid, experimental or still developing. Their communities deserve room to define their own goals, rather than having their identity dismissed by an outside charter.

We do not ask anyone to support deception. AI-generated images should not be passed off as genuine photographs of completed buildings or events. The original Charter is right to raise those concerns, and it explicitly permits a range of AI-assisted creative tasks. Our disagreement is specifically with exclusionary definitions and the suggestion that one external framework should settle what a micronation may be.

Micronationalism should be a freedom to create — not a permission slip issued by other micronations.

WHAT WE'RE ASKING

A better, more inclusive charter.

01

Reconsider Article V

Remove or revise the blanket distinction that classifies entirely virtual nation-building as geofiction rather than micronationalism.

↗
02

Respect each nation's legislature

Recognise each nation's Legislative Assembly — or constitutionally authorised lawmaking institution — as the body that decides whether and how AI may be used in its government.

↗
03

Keep ethical standards voluntary

Encourage transparency and accountability without presenting a charter signed by some micronations as a universal test of legitimacy for all.

↗
04

Protect honest creativity

Distinguish digital proposals, artwork, virtual infrastructure and AI-assisted administration from attempts to deceive the public about actual achievements.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Before you sign.

Does this petition oppose all AI rules?

No. The petition calls for national legislative independence, clear distinctions between digital concepts and real-world claims, and reconsideration of Article V. It does not oppose responsible safeguards against deliberate deception.

Does signing mean my micronation officially endorses this?

Not automatically. A representative may submit a signature while claiming to be authorised. Signatures are subject to moderation. Any formal government endorsement should also follow that nation's own procedures.

Can individuals sign if they do not represent a nation?

Yes. Choose “Individual supporter” on the form. Signatures from individuals and micronational representatives are counted together, but distinguished on the public supporter list.

Why might my name not be visible?

Signatures count immediately. Only supporters who choose to be displayed publicly appear in the visible list.

Will you publish my email address?

No. Email addresses are stored privately for administration and duplicate prevention. Only the name, nation or affiliation, and role you elect to display may appear on the public supporter list.
